What the contest is about

The Ganesh Chaturthi Game Design Contest invites students to build a game or a fun, game-based interactive application. The idea is entirely up to them. Each team picks something it would genuinely like to build, turns it into a working product, and lets students from different campuses play it.

The organizers are not looking for the biggest project or the most complicated code. A small game that feels complete and is fun to replay can do better than a large game that is unfinished, much like a small, lovingly shaped clay murti can mean more than a grand one left half made.

Build a Vinayaka Chaturthi game

Every game must be themed around Vinayaka Chaturthi. The festival is the heart of the contest, so each entry should clearly connect to Lord Ganesha, his stories, or the way the festival is celebrated. Games with no link to the festival will not be considered.

The theme is fixed, but the type of game is each team's choice. A runner, puzzle, quiz, memory, arcade, rhythm or story game all work, as long as the festival is at its centre.

Teams can draw from festival elements such as:

  • Lord Ganesha's stories
  • Modaks and festive sweets
  • Mushak, Ganesha's mouse
  • Pandals and decorations
  • Rangoli patterns
  • Dhol and procession music
  • Puja, durva and flowers
  • Visarjan
  • Eco-friendly clay murtis

A few game ideas to get started

  • Modak catch. Catch falling modaks in a basket before the plate fills up.
  • Mushak dash. An endless runner starring Ganesha's little mouse companion.
  • Pandal puzzle. Assemble a pandal decoration piece by piece against the clock.
  • Rangoli memory. Match rangoli patterns before the colours fade.
  • Dhol rhythm. Tap in time with the procession beat and build a combo.
  • Eco murti maker. Choose natural materials and score points for a greener celebration.
  • Vinayaka stories quiz. Answer questions on Ganesha's stories and festival traditions.
  • Visarjan journey. Guide the procession safely through the streets to the water.
  • Puja collector. Gather durva, flowers and offerings before the aarti begins.

These are only starting points. Teams are free to create their own festival idea.

Handle the theme with respect. Lord Ganesha should always be shown with care. Games should not show him being hurt, attacked or mocked.

What every game should have

The idea can be anything, but the final game should feel complete. Before submitting, each team makes sure the basics are covered.

Easy to startA new player quickly understands what to do.
Working gameplayThe main game loop works without major bugs.
Clear resultA score, time, level, win or loss, progress or another clear outcome is shown.
Proper endingThe player knows when a round or game is finished.
Play againPlayers can restart and try again.
Mobile and laptopThe final version works on common mobile and laptop or desktop browsers.
Cross-campus accessStudents from every participating campus can open and play it easily, with no campus-specific access restrictions.
Safe contentThe game stays suitable for students, with nothing offensive, hateful, explicit or disrespectful.

How the contest will run

  1. RegisterStudents join individually or as a team, either solo or in teams of up to 4.
  2. Choose an ideaEach team decides what game it wants to build. There is no fixed genre.
  3. Build and testTeams create the game, test it properly and make sure the basic flow works.
  4. SubmitTeams share the game link, code and a short demo before 20 September, 5:00 PM.
  5. Play across campusesApproved games are opened for students from participating campuses to play.
  6. Demo and judgingShortlisted teams may be asked to show their game and explain how they built it.
  7. ResultsWinners are chosen by judging score and announced by the organizers.

Leaderboards and cross-campus play

Games are meant to be played, not just submitted. After the build round, approved games are shared across campuses so students can try them and compete.

  • Players can play a game more than once.
  • For a score-based game, a player's best valid score can appear on the leaderboard.
  • If a game is based on time, levels or another result, that result can be used instead of a score.
  • Each game has its own ranking, because different games use different scoring systems.
  • All game leaderboards may be shown on one common dashboard with campus and game filters.
  • Raw scores from completely different games are not combined unless the organizers announce a fair common scoring method.
No natural score? That's fine. The team adds a simple measurable result such as completion time, level reached, number of wins, stars collected, or any other result that makes sense for the game.

What makes a game stand out

The actual playing experience matters most, not the amount of code written.

Fun and playabilityIt's enjoyable, and players want to play again.
CreativityThe game has its own idea, twist or personality.
CompletenessThe full flow works from start to result and restart.
Ease of use and lookControls, screens and feedback are easy to understand.
Technical qualityIt runs smoothly and avoids major bugs.
Demo and explanationThe team can clearly show the game and explain what it built.

Simple rules

  • Entries must be games or fun, game-based interactive applications. General utility apps, dashboards, business tools and other non-game projects will not be considered.
  • The submitted game must be functional. If the link does not open or the main gameplay does not work during evaluation, the entry may not be evaluated.
  • Each team builds its own game. Libraries, starter code and development tools are allowed, but copied or plagiarized projects, or someone else's complete game, are not.
  • Coding assistants and AI tools may be used if the organizers allow them. The team must still understand the code and be able to explain the important parts.
  • Teams use only images, music, characters and other assets they have permission to use.
  • No offensive, explicit, hateful, discriminatory, or culturally or religiously disrespectful content. Festival themes, in particular, should be handled with care and respect.
  • Games must not collect passwords, payment information or unnecessary personal data from players.
  • Faking scores, automating score submissions or manipulating the leaderboard is not allowed.
  • Teams submit before the announced deadline and keep their game link working throughout the contest.
  • If judges ask for a code walkthrough, the team should be able to explain how the game works.
